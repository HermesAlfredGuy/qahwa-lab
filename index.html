01 — The idea

We don't invent flavour. We climb for it.

Khawlani is the oldest coffee in Arabia and the hardest to get right. It grows in the fog line of Jazan's escarpment, on terraces cut by hand, and it is dried whole on rooftops before it ever meets a drum. Our work is small on purpose: keep the mountain in the cup, and take it out of the way.

02 — The mountain

Grown where the cloud sits.

Above 1,600 metres the air is cold, the light is hard, and the cherries ripen slowly — three weeks later than the valley floor. Smallholders in the Jazan highlands pick by hand, float the fruit in spring water, then spread it on flat rooftops to dry in the sun for twenty-one days.

We buy the same lots every harvest, by name, and pay above the farm-gate price. No blending across villages: if the cup changes, the lot label changes.

03 — The roast

Eleven minutes, forty seconds.

01

Charge

Green beans hit a drum pre-heated to 200 °C. The temperature drops to 92 °C — the turning point. From here every second is logged by hand and by probe.

02

First crack

At 8:10 the beans snap audibly as moisture escapes. We hold development short — 3 minutes 30 — so the jasmine and dried fig of the highlands survive the caramel.

03

Drop & rest

Dropped at 212 °C, cooled in four minutes, rested 48 hours in one-way valves. Nothing leaves the lab before it has stopped changing.
